## 文章

**Animals everywhere on Earth are affected by human noise**

NOISE pollution is reshaping the lives of an abundance of animals living on Earth — very few places are not subject to human-caused noise pollution — and the effects are comparable to other human activities. In the oceans, for example, ambient noise levels have doubled every 10 years since 1950. Now, scientists are learning more about the wide-ranging and widespread impacts of the din.

A meta-analysis published in the journal Science on 19 November looked at 177 aquatic and terrestrial species. It concluded that noise pollution altered at least one physiological, behavioural or ecological trait in 80 per cent of the species studied, with the effects consistently negative. "Every ecosystem, or every major habitat, is affected by noise," says Helmut Kunc at Queen's University Belfast in the UK, who conducted the research with his colleague Hansjoerg Kunc. "We looked at a lot of different physiological and biological aspects. Noise causes animals to work 2.5 times harder to catch 25 per cent less food. For example, in noisy conditions, predators can spend 2.5 times harder to catch 25 per cent less prey (??). Creatures like bats may expend more energy and are less successful at hunting when subjected to ambient noise. For some species, noise disrupts communication and disorientates marine life such as whales and dolphins.

"Animals avoid noisy habitats, which shrinks their available area and therefore limits population sizes." Animals also learn to avoid noisy areas, which can shrink their available area and therefore limit their population size.

**Noisy ship engines disrupt navigation in dolphins**

Some animals avoid noisy coastlines, while bats have been shown to avoid foraging in areas that experienced noise pollution, for example, from motorbike rallies long after the events end. In noise-polluted areas, mortality among animals starts to creep up, on average (??) per cent. Sometimes, the death is instantaneous — consider the harm from noise blasts during oil and gas exploration, for example, can cause death. Other times, it is slower and more painful, such as starvation from reduced hunting success.

Not every animal suffers as a result of anthropogenic noise, Kunc notes. Some predators actually benefit from the din of human noise flushing their prey out of hiding, leading to more successful hunting.

But even though some animals might benefit in the short term, the overall effect of noise is negative on the ecosystem, he says. The animals that thrive are being warped by external stresses. "If you think about how this ecosystem should be adapted to the way it should be," he says. "So in my mind, it doesn't really matter who is more or less affected. It always is and that is what we want to avoid, because we want a functioning ecosystem."

Kunc wants more thought to be given to the soundscape of the natural world, alongside the conservation of plants and animals. He argues that the acoustic environment is a dynamic evolutionary force, and we must appreciate that anthropogenic noise changes the physiology, development and behaviour of life.

### Q1（主旨）
This article is mainly about:
- A. how bats hunt for food at night
- B. how human noise widely and negatively affects animals on Earth
- C. how to build quieter ships
- D. how satellites take photos

**答案：** B
**文中依據：** 標題＋「noise pollution altered at least one…trait in 80 per cent of the species studied, with the effects consistently negative」
**💡技巧：** 主旨題睇標題＋研究結論句。
**⚠️陷阱：** A 只係一個例子；D 係同版另一篇文章，唔關事。

### Q2（細節）
How much have ambient noise levels in the oceans risen since 1950?
- A. they have stayed the same
- B. they have doubled every 10 years
- C. they have halved every 10 years

**文中依據：** 「ambient noise levels have doubled every 10 years since 1950」

### Q3（細節）
How many species were looked at in the meta-analysis?
- A. 80
